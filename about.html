BHAKSHI is a small Indian label making the shirts we grew up around — piqué polos, rugby stripes and soft knits — in small runs, with the care going into the details.

The story

We make the collared shirts we kept reaching for: a polo whose collar holds its shape, a rugby with stripes that feel worn-in from day one, a knit that works from a monsoon evening to a Sunday lunch.

The college polo, the club jersey, the knit your father wore on weekends — classics that have always looked right here. We redraw them with cleaner colours, a relaxed fit and small details: contrast collars, sleeve tipping, stripes and script lettering.

No seasons and no catalogue of hundreds of styles. A few pieces at a time, made properly, sold directly to you and confirmed one-on-one on WhatsApp.

How a polo is made

Three stages, each one done carefully. Most of what makes a good polo is decided before anyone sees the colour.

  1. 01

    Knit

    It starts with the fabric. Cotton yarn is knitted into piqué, rib, waffle or heavy jersey — each chosen for how it breathes, how it drapes and how well it holds a collar.

  2. 02

    Cut & sew

    The knit is cut to a relaxed pattern with room through the chest, then sewn up with a clean placket and sleeves set to sit at the right length.

  3. 03

    Collar & finish

    The collar goes on last and matters most. Then come the details — stripes, tipping, embroidery or a raised script — and every piece is checked for stitching and measurements before it's folded and packed.

Why small runs

Every style is made in a fixed run — a few dozen pieces, not thousands.

Small runs let us spend time where it shows: the weight of a collar, the spacing of a stripe, the exact colour of the tipping. And when something isn't quite right, we can catch it early.

They also mean we make close to what people actually buy — less dead stock, fewer unsold boxes. When a run sells out, that's usually it. We'd rather make something new than repeat the same polo forever.
